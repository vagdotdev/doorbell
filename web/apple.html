Video calls are appointments. Friendship never was.

In a hostel, you walk down the corridor and knock. You barge in. Someone yells from the hallway. Nobody sends a calendar invite for maggi at 2 a.m. Doorbell is that corridor, living in your Mac’s notch. No links. No scheduling. No meetings.

The knock

Someone’s at the door.

Two kinds of glass. Same door.

Click a friend. That’s the knock. Your camera and mic come on, and you’re standing at their door under the porch light.

On their Mac, the notch becomes a peephole. Your face, small. Your voice, low, as if through the door. They can press Listen to turn you up.

No ringtone. No red button. A soft two-note chime, then one green button to let you in. Or they leave the door shut. That’s an answer too.

Close friends

Close friends don’t knock.
They walk in.

The room

Privacy

What happens at the door stays at the door.

No green dots.

No online status, no “active now,” no last seen. Nobody knows who’s home.

No read receipts.

No seen. No delivered. No “peeked and didn’t answer.” Silence can always mean not there.

Quiet Door.

One switch in Settings. A knock shrinks to a pinhole — a tiny face in the notch, no sound, no bounce. They never know you were busy.

Chat that doesn’t stick around.

Messages live in the room. When you leave, they’re gone.

Doorbell for Mac. Apple silicon, macOS 15 or later.

curl -fsSL https://doorbellnotch.vercel.app/install.sh | zsh

Paste it into Terminal. It downloads the latest release, checks the checksum and signature, and installs Doorbell in Applications.

Doorbell needs your camera and microphone to knock and answer, and Screen Recording to share your screen. The app checks for updates on its own, so you usually won’t need the update command.
